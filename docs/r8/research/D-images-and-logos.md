# Category D: NBA Player Headshots and Team Logos, Legitimate Sources

Prepared for: Pivot (NBA decision-intelligence product)
Retrieval date for every source below: **2026-10-18** (unless stated otherwise)
Status: research only. Nothing downloaded except HTTP headers (see section 5). This is not legal advice. Items marked **[COUNSEL]** need a sports/IP lawyer before any public launch.

**Bottom line up front.** There is no free, public, redistributable source for current NBA headshots or for the NBA team logos as trademarks. The NBA owns the league and team marks and its photo library. Getty Images (as "NBAE") is the exclusive photo distributor, and its own NBA terms say NBA imagery may not be licensed without prior approval, and that storing or redistributing it needs separate written approval from NBA Entertainment. Player likeness is a separate right again (NBPA group licensing / individual players). The "public" CDNs (cdn.nba.com, a.espncdn.com) are reachable but are not licensed to Pivot. The only genuinely free-licensed material is a patchy subset of Wikimedia Commons player photos (CC BY / BY-SA, attribution required) and a few simple logos that Wikipedia tags as public-domain for copyright purposes (still trademarks). A commercial-grade answer requires a conversation with NBA Properties / NBA Entertainment (and Getty) plus the NBPA's group-licensing arm.

A personal, noncommercial download to one computer is allowed with notices kept. Posting or using materials on other sites or social media for public or commercial purposes needs written permission. The NBA name/logo, team names/logos, uniform trade dress, and game photos are the property of NBA Properties, the leagues, or teams, and no implied license is granted. Images of people are used with permission and unauthorized use may implicate publicity and privacy law. Statistics (Section 9) require attribution and are limited to news reporting or private noncommercial use; sponsorship, gambling, fantasy, real-time/play-by-play products and databases of regularly updated stats need express consent. The terms did not explicitly mention scraping or CDNs. **Verified-from-page (summarized by the fetch tool; read the primary text before relying).**
- **CDN headers** (single-URL HEAD checks, 2026-10-18; see section 5): `cdn.nba.com/headshots/nba/latest/1040x760/2544.png` returned HTTP 200, `image/png`, ~198 KB, `cache-control: private, no-transform, max-age=43200`, last-modified 2026-10-03. `cdn.nba.com/logos/nba/1610612747/primary/L/logo.svg` returned HTTP 200, SVG, last-modified 2020-09-02. ## 3. Trademark, right-of-publicity, editorial vs commercial: considerations (not legal advice)

All of this is general background from my own knowledge, not from fetched sources this session. **[COUNSEL]** for every point.

1. **Logos are trademarks and copyrighted artwork.** Source terms above (NBA ToU; Wikipedia's own file pages) both state this. Nominative fair use can permit using a team's name to refer to the team, but it generally covers only as much of the mark as necessary and must not suggest sponsorship or endorsement. Whether it covers using the full-color logo graphic, at scale, as a UI element on every row of a commercial product is more contested than using the plain-text team name. Expect the NBA to take the position that logos need a license (its ToU says so).
2. **Player names vs likenesses.** Publishing factual statistics with player names is a different legal question from using photographs. Right-of-publicity law is state-by-state (e.g. New York, California statutes and common law) and focuses on commercial exploitation of a person's identity (advertising/trade). Past court cases have often favored use of names and stats in news/information contexts, but pictures used to attract paid subscribers are riskier, and a subscription analytics product is commercial. Getty's NBA terms and the Sportradar addenda explicitly carve out player NIL and say licensees must get their own rights. The NBPA exists to license group player rights.
3. **Editorial vs commercial.** Vendors treat "editorial" as news/illustrative use and "commercial" as advertising, promotion, merchandising, gambling. A paid analytics product sits in a gray zone: it is informational, but monetized and likely to be seen as promotional if it uses images in marketing, onboarding, or social posts. Never use any photos in marketing without separate clearance. Betting-adjacent features compound restrictions (NBA ToU stats clause, Sportradar/Getty restrictions on gambling/betting use).
4. **Team colors.** Color hex values are facts and generally unprotectable as such; color schemes can function as trademark/trade dress in combination with names and logos. A generic badge using a team abbreviation (e.g., "BOS") in approximately-that-team's color is lower risk than using logos, but "lower risk" is not "cleared", and SportsDataIO notes that even its color fields are not licensed for public or commercial use. Treat colors as a **[COUNSEL]** check; a conservative approach is to use a neutral per-team palette (or only a dot/stripe of the primary color) rather than faithful reproduction.
5. **Caching/hosting.** Getty/NBAE terms require separate written NBA approval to store or redistribute. SportsDataIO and Sportradar docs allow caching of API data for the licensed product (SportsDataIO FAQ explicitly; Sportradar FAQ recommends it), but Sportradar's image rights depend on provider terms. Hosting on your own CDN needs to be written into any agreement.
6. **Attribution.** Commons: creator name, license, link. Getty/NBAE: fixed credit line. Sportradar/SportsDataIO: per contract. NBA stats require attribution to NBA.com per NBA ToU Section 9.
7. **Takedown exposure.** NBA Properties actively enforces against unauthorized use (e.g., Schedule A litigation results appeared in search). Not verified further.

## 4. Recommendation in three tiers

### Tier (a): Safe development-only (now)
- Keep local `nbalogos/` and `nbaplayerimages/` **gitignored, never committed, never pushed to a public host, never in Docker images or screenshots shared externally**. Add a repo note that provenance is unverified and the folders are for local development only. Don't serve them from any publicly reachable backend (including preview deployments).
- Make the fallback the *default* for anything not local: **monogram/initials circle** for players; **generated SVG badges** for teams (3-letter abbreviation, neutral or approximate color, system font; no logo shapes, no wordmarks, no mascot). Colors: low risk but not cleared; see Section 3.4. Use text team names (nominative reference), not logos.
- Build the UI so image source is a pluggable provider (`ImageProvider` returns URL + credit string + license tag + expiry). That lets a licensed provider, a Commons provider, or the fallback be swapped in with no UI rewrite, and lets you display attribution when required.
- Record provenance per file where known (source URL/date) so you can later tell what is replaceable.

### Tier (b): Private / invite-only beta
- Start from (a). Default to monograms/badges for everything shown to outside users. Do not display NBA-sourced photos or logos to people outside the team, including via login-gated screens, until you have written permission; a gate does not remove the display/distribution right issue (this reading is mine; **[COUNSEL]**).
- Optional free layer: **Wikimedia Commons photos with automated attribution** for players who have a suitable CC BY / BY-SA image, caching locally (not hotlinking), showing creator + license + link in a small credit, skipping any file lacking a clear free license. Expect partial, uneven-quality coverage (see 2.8) and no logos. Right-of-publicity is not solved by the license, so consider limiting this to a small closed beta and getting counsel's view first.
- In parallel, request quotes/trials: SportsDataIO (headshot add-on) and Sportradar Images (30-day trial; logos need sales). Use trial keys only in private dev with trial restrictions, and do not cache beyond what the trial terms allow (not yet known).
- Beta terms: add a clear "unofficial; not affiliated with or endorsed by the NBA, its teams, or players" notice. Avoid any marketing screenshots with logos/photos.

### Tier (c): Production commercial: what is realistically required
Expect **three separate rights holders / layers**:
1. **Marks (team logos, NBA logo, wordmarks, uniforms):** NBA Properties (the NBA's marketing/licensing arm, per search result, 645 Fifth Avenue NY per a third-party directory that may be stale). Request a license (likely a digital/app or data-product license) or a written no-objection to defined nominative use. Teams own their marks via NBA Properties.
2. **Photos (headshots/action):** NBA Entertainment / NBAE, distributed via Getty Images (collection terms: prior approval, no storage without NBA Entertainment approval, credit line, 30-day window for editorial web). Alternatively, a data vendor whose photos are licensed to you for your specific use (SportsDataIO / Sportradar-Getty/Imagn), with the vendor confirming in writing that public web display, roster-wide, cached/hosted, subscription-product use is permitted.
3. **Player likeness:** NBPA's group-licensing arm (PLYRS UNTD, name to be confirmed with the NBPA) and/or the individual players. A product showing many players' photos probably falls in group-licensing territory; this is where timeline and cost are most uncertain.

Realistic expectations (my judgment, not sourced): bespoke negotiation with multi-week to multi-month timelines; fees and minimum guarantees likely scale with revenue and use case (public pricing does not exist for any layer I found); monetized analytics with betting adjacency is the hardest category; startups often launch on text-only identification plus abstract badges until revenue justifies licensing. **[COUNSEL]** to scope whether a photo-free launch is clearly safe.

Who to contact (departments, no emails invented):
- NBA Properties / NBA licensing and business affairs (route via nba.com corporate; I found no public intake page — ask for the NBA's digital licensing / data-partnerships contact).
- NBA Entertainment photo licensing: begin via Getty Images' sports/NBA licensing team (Getty's collection-restrictions page directs licensees to their local Getty office).
- NBPA group licensing (PLYRS UNTD / NBPA licensing department): via nbpa.com.
- Vendors: SportsDataIO sales (headshots page directs here); Sportradar Images sales (and mediasupport per FAQ).
- Optional: the NBA's official data partners, to see how Pivot-like products are structured.
