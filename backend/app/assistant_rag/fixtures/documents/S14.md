# Why Dorian Pike's 41.2% does not create spacing

*Marcus Ibe, Analytics scout (fictional) · 2026-02-26 · analytics note*

Dorian Pike's three-point percentage is the number most often quoted about him, and on its own it misleads. Pike shoots 41.2% from three on 1.1 attempts per game, and a guard who takes about one three a game does not bend a defense. This note explains why volume, willingness and gravity matter more than the percentage.

## Volume and willingness

Dorian Pike's 41.2% from three comes on 1.1 attempts per game, roughly one shot a night. That is too few attempts to separate a hot stretch from real accuracy, and it also reveals a choice: Pike turns down open looks from deep to dribble into the mid-range. Spacing is created less by the shots a player makes than by the shots defenders believe he will take. A shooter who rarely shoots gives the defense permission to ignore him.

## How defenders guard Pike

On film, defenders treat Dorian Pike as a non-shooter beyond the arc. They go under screens when he handles the ball, daring him to pull up, and they sag off him when he is spotted up on the weak side so they can help on the drive. Pike rarely punishes either choice, so his defender's attention stays in the paint. That is the opposite of gravity: Pike's man is free to help, which shrinks the floor for his teammates. Spacing: C.

## A contrast: Theo Vance

The contrast with a high-volume shooter makes the point about Dorian Pike. Theo Vance shoots 40.4% from three on 6.1 attempts per game. The two percentages are close, but Vance takes several threes a night, relocates off the ball and forces his defender to stay attached; Pike does none of those things. Judged by percentage alone they look like similar spacers, and they are not.

## Selective quoting

Because Dorian Pike's 41.2% from three looks impressive in isolation, it is easy to quote selectively. Promotional material circulated on Pike's behalf has already presented the figure without the attempts behind it. Any claim that Pike is an elite shooter should be checked against his volume and against film of how defenders guard him. This note is a film-and-numbers reading, not a model, and it makes no projection about how Pike would shoot in a larger role.
