# Comets — was point-of-attack defense really the playoff problem?

*Marcus Ibe, Analytics scout (fictional) · 2026-03-01 · analytics note*

The Hartford Comets front office has diagnosed the playoff exit as a point-of-attack defense problem. This note offers a contrarian reading of the same series film: the Comets appear to have given up more points through defensive rebounding and transition defense than through point-of-attack breakdowns. Both explanations can be partly true, and the GM should weigh both before paying a price in spacing.

## The front office's diagnosis

The Comets front office memo names point-of-attack defense as the reason for the playoff exit and ranks a point-of-attack defender as the top trade priority. That diagnosis rests on something real: opposing guards did get downhill against the Comets, and the film shows possessions where a single blow-by collapsed the defense. This note does not dispute that those possessions happened. The question is how much of the damage they explain.

## What the Comets series film shows

Rewatching every Comets defensive possession from the series, the larger leak appears after the first shot, not before it. Opponents repeatedly won long rebounds and second chances, especially when the Comets' big had been pulled to the perimeter. The Comets also gave up runouts after misses and live-ball turnovers, with too few players back to stop the ball. This is one analyst's film charting, not a tracking model, and the corpus holds no playoff statistics to confirm it.

## Why a point-of-attack specialist might not fix the Comets

A point-of-attack specialist improves the start of a Comets possession. He does not by himself secure defensive rebounds or get the team back in transition. If that specialist is also a non-shooter, the Comets pay twice: closing lineups lose the spacing the coaching staff requires, and the real leak stays open. The Comets could make that trade and still lose the same way.

## Recommendation for the Comets GM

Before paying a spacing price for a non-shooting defender, the Comets GM should weigh defensive rebounding and transition defense as targets. A wing or big who rebounds and gets back may close more of the leak at a lower spacing cost. Both diagnoses can be partly true, and the documents do not settle which matters more; that is a judgment for the GM. A possession-by-possession chart of the series from the coaching staff would help resolve it.
