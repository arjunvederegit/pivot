# Hartford Comets — playoff defense priorities

*Ruth Abernathy, Comets front office (fictional) · 2026-01-15 · team strategy memo*

This memo sets the Hartford Comets front office's priorities for the trade market after our playoff exit. The Comets are a contender built on five-out spacing around our lead guard, and our timeline is this season's playoffs. Our diagnosis is that the series was lost at the point of attack, and every trade target must be evaluated against the needs below.

## Where the Comets stand

The Hartford Comets are built to contend now, with five-out spacing around our lead guard, who handles most of the creation. Rafael Sorensen is our stretch big: he pulls the opposing center away from the rim and is central to that spacing. Niko Valdez is our backup point guard. Both are already on the roster; neither is a trade target, and this memo is about what the Comets add around them.

## Diagnosis: point-of-attack defense

The Comets' playoff exit came when opposing guards repeatedly got downhill. Our perimeter defenders could not stay in front of the ball or get over screens, so our help collapsed into the paint and the kick-outs found open shooters. The front office's diagnosis is that point-of-attack defense was the core problem. To be candid, this is the front office's reading of the series film, not a measured finding, and we have asked the analytics staff to test it.

## Comets trade priorities

The Comets front office ranks its needs in this order:

- **First: a point-of-attack defender.** A guard who can pressure the ball, fight over screens and keep opposing guards out of the lane.
- **Second: a switchable wing defender.** A wing who can switch across positions without conceding a mismatch.
- **Third: do not sacrifice spacing.** Our closing lineups need shooters, and a defender who cannot shoot shrinks the floor around our lead guard.

Secondary playmaking is a nice-to-have for the Comets, because our lead guard handles creation. Rebounding is a lower priority.

## Open questions for the Comets

Every Comets trade target must be evaluated against these needs, not a reputation or a single statistic. The front office has not yet decided how much spacing it would trade for defense: the best point-of-attack defenders available may not be shooters, and the coaching staff's closing-lineup requirements limit how far we can bend. Cap questions are handled outside this memo.
