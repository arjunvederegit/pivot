# Method guide — spacing is not a percentage

*Pivot Scouting Methods, Methods desk (fictional) · 2025-10-01 · method guide*

This method guide explains how Pivot scouts evaluate spacing: whether a defender has to stay attached to a player away from the ball. Spacing is not a three-point percentage. Read this guide before quoting any shooter's percentage in a fit discussion.

## The spacing equation
Pivot scouts treat spacing as volume × accuracy × willingness × movement. Volume is how often a player shoots from three; accuracy is how often those shots go in; willingness is whether he lets it fly against a late closeout instead of passing it up; movement is whether he relocates and runs off screens so a defender must chase him. Because the factors multiply, spacing collapses when any one of them is close to zero.

## Why a high percentage on low volume does not create spacing
High accuracy on very low volume does not force a defender to stay attached. When a player takes about one three a game, the defense goes under screens, sags into the paint, helps off him and lives with the occasional make. Low-volume percentages also swing on a handful of shots. For spacing purposes, treat an eye-catching percentage on light volume as an open question about willingness, not as proof of gravity.

## Corner threes, above-the-break threes and playoff willingness
Location matters for spacing too. Corner shooters stretch the baseline and pull the low help defender away from the rim; above-the-break shooters who fire off movement or the dribble force their defender to fight over screens, which creates more gravity. Playoff defenses then test willingness: they leave the weakest shooter open on purpose and dare him to shoot. Spacing value disappears if a shooter hesitates or passes up those looks, whatever his regular-season percentage.

## Reading the 3P% and 3PA columns together
On Pivot's stat sheets, never read the three-point percentage column (3P%) without the three-point attempts column (3PA) beside it. Read the attempts first: a low attempts-per-game figure means the percentage rests on very few shots and says little about spacing. Then read the accuracy. Finally, check the scouting reports for willingness and movement, which no stat sheet records. When a percentage is quoted without its volume, as in promotional material, treat the spacing claim as incomplete.
