# Albuquerque Sundogs — offense upgrade plan

*Lena Vasquez, Sundogs front office (fictional) · 2026-02-08 · team strategy memo*

The Albuquerque Sundogs win with defense, but our half-court offense stalls, and this memo sets the front office's plan to fix it. We need shooting volume and secondary creation, we will accept some defensive drop-off to get them, and we will keep the team-defense identity that makes the Sundogs a playoff team.

## Sundogs identity: defense first

The Albuquerque Sundogs are a defense-first team. Bastian Holloway sets the tone at the point of attack, pressuring the ball and disrupting actions before they start, and Dante Cruz protects the rim behind him. That combination is the core of our team defense: ball handlers are funneled toward Dante Cruz, and the rotations behind them define us. Whatever changes on offense, the front office considers the Sundogs' team-defense identity something we must preserve.

## Where the Sundogs offense stalls

The Sundogs' half-court offense stalls because opponents can ignore too many of our players. Defenders sag off our non-shooters and fill the paint, so drives run into crowds and kick-outs go to players the defense is happy to leave open. When our first action is taken away, too few Sundogs can attack a closeout or make the next read on the second side, and too many possessions end in a contested late-clock shot.

## What the Sundogs need

The Sundogs front office is targeting two things: shooting volume and secondary creation. Shooting volume means players who take threes often enough that defenders have to stay attached, not a good percentage on a few attempts. Secondary creation means a player who can attack closeouts and keep the ball moving when the first option is denied. We will accept some defensive drop-off, but not a player who breaks our rotations: a weaker on-ball defender who is a sound team defender is acceptable.

## Bastian Holloway and the Sundogs' choice

The Sundogs are open to discussing Bastian Holloway if a deal returns shooting. We also know Holloway's defense is central to who we are, and moving him would change the team's identity more than any other move. Any Holloway conversation has to bring back real shooting and leave the team defense around Dante Cruz intact. The front office has not decided to move him, and contract questions are handled outside this memo.
