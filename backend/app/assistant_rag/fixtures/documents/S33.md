# Coaching clinic — switching schemes and wing size

*Pivot Coaching Clinic, Coaching education desk (fictional) · 2025-12-05 · coaching note*

This coaching clinic note covers when a switching defense works, why perimeter size matters in playoff switching, and what switching costs a team whose center is slow-footed. It is general coaching guidance on defensive schemes, not an evaluation of any player.

## When switching works
Switching works when the defenders exchanging assignments are close enough in size and quickness that the switch creates no mismatch. Wings of similar size who can guard one through four let a defense switch most screens without giving up a driving lane or a post-up. Switching also depends on communication: switches must be called early and loudly, and the defender releasing his man must know who is picking up the screener.

## Why perimeter size matters in playoff switching
In playoff switching, opponents run the same actions again and again to find the switch they want. Small guards switched onto big wings get posted up; slow defenders switched onto quick guards get beaten off the dribble. Perimeter size and length shrink those mismatches, which is why switch-heavy playoff defenses prize long wings who can contest shots and hold position against bigger players.

## The weak-link problem in switching schemes
One weak link breaks a switching scheme. Playoff offenses hunt the weakest defender by screening with his man until he is switched onto their best creator, then clearing the floor. The defense must either keep switching and concede the mismatch or abandon the scheme by helping, trapping or hedging, which leaves shooters open elsewhere. Coaching staffs should identify who will be hunted before a series, not during it.

## Switching versus drop coverage for slow-footed centers
Slow-footed centers are the hardest switching problem. Switched onto a guard in space, a big who lacks lateral quickness concedes drives or fouls. Drop coverage keeps the center near the rim, where his size and rebounding help, at the cost of conceding pull-up jumpers above the screen. Teams with a slow-footed center usually switch with the wings and drop with the big, accepting that trade-off rather than forcing him to guard on the perimeter.
