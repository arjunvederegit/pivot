# Comets coaching staff — closing-lineup requirements

*Hollis Grant, Comets assistant coach (fictional) · 2026-02-03 · coaching note*

This note records what the Hartford Comets coaching staff needs from a closing lineup after the playoff series. It is the staff's requirement, written for the front office. It describes the lineups we believe we can win with late in playoff games; it is not a decision about any trade, which belongs to the front office.

## Four credible shooters around the center

The Comets coaching staff's first requirement is that a closing lineup has four credible shooters around the center. In the playoff series, defenses ignored our non-shooters and loaded up on our lead guard: a second defender came at the ball, a helper sat in the lane, and the non-shooter was dared to beat us. By credible we mean a shooter the defense has to stay attached to, a player who takes threes willingly and often, not one with a good percentage on a handful of attempts.

## Defensive requirements for the Comets

On defense, the Comets staff wants a guard who can pressure the ball and fight over screens, so opposing guards cannot get downhill the way they did in the series. We also want wings who can switch one through four without creating a mismatch we have to help on. Defensive communication matters as much as foot speed: calling screens early, talking through switches and pointing out the weak-side shooter. One quiet defender breaks a switching scheme.

## Non-shooters in the Comets rotation

A non-shooter can play the first half for the Comets, especially if his defense wins those minutes. He is hard to close with. Late in a playoff game the staff would have to choose between his defense and the spacing our lead guard needs, and in the series that choice cost us. If the front office adds a non-shooting defender, the staff would plan for him as a starter or a situational defensive substitute, not a closer.

## What this note does not decide

This is the Comets coaching staff's requirement, not the front office's decision. The staff has not evaluated specific trade targets here, and how much spacing to give up for defense is the front office's call. Any candidate's shooting volume and defense should be checked against the scouting reports rather than inferred from this note.
